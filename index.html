Core service

Oil Purification, Filtration & Vacuum Dehydration

The core of F&F Oil Solutions.

Our core service is on-site oil purification. It combines particle filtration and vacuum dehydration to treat suitable hydraulic, lubricating, turbine, and compressor oils at your facility, and to reclaim oil that can stay in service.

Filtration targets solid contamination. Vacuum dehydration targets free and dissolved water and helps release entrained air and gases. Together, these processes can help recover usable oil and support cleaner operating conditions.

Treatment suitability depends on the oil type, condition, contamination, and equipment requirements. Reclamation does not reverse every form of oil degradation or restore depleted additives.

Applications we evaluate

  • Hydraulic power units and reservoirs.
  • Hydraulic presses and stamping equipment.
  • Plastic injection molding and die casting equipment.
  • Compatible industrial lubrication systems.
  • Turbine and compressor lube oil systems, reviewed individually.
  • Other oil-filled industrial systems reviewed individually.

Benefits

  • Help extend the useful life of suitable oil.
  • Reduce water and particle contamination.
  • Support equipment reliability.
  • Help reduce avoidable oil replacement and disposal.
  • Support maintenance decisions with oil condition information.

Where the equipment design and site conditions allow, treatment may be performed while the machine remains in operation. We assess connection requirements and operating conditions before confirming the service approach.

High-Velocity Oil Flushing

Clean the circuit. Support the next start-up.

High-velocity oil flushing circulates a compatible fluid through an agreed system or circuit at flow rates above normal operating flow. The turbulent flow helps lift particles and residual contamination from piping and components so our filtration equipment can capture them.

Projects may include new installations and pre-commissioning, maintenance work, component replacement, or contamination recovery. Flow requirements, fluid compatibility, circuit configuration, and cleanliness targets, such as an ISO 4406 code, are reviewed before the service is defined.

Potential applications

  • Hydraulic piping and circuits.
  • Lubrication piping and systems.
  • Turbine and compressor lube oil systems.
  • New system start-up and pre-commissioning.
  • Selected industrial oil circuits.
  • Suitable systems at manufacturing, energy, and oil and gas facilities.

Each proposal defines the circuits to be flushed, flushing fluid, fluid supply responsibility, connection points, operating requirements, and acceptance criteria.

Fuel Filtration & Water Removal

Clean, dry fuel for the engines you depend on.

Water and particles in stored diesel can plug filters, wear fuel injectors, and lead to unplanned stops. We filter fuel in tanks and supply systems with high-efficiency filter elements combined with coalescer and separator elements that remove free water.

Service can be scheduled for storage tanks, day tanks, standby generator fuel, and equipment fleets. Fuel sampling can be included to compare condition before and after treatment.

Potential applications

  • Diesel for standby and prime power generators.
  • Fuel storage tanks and day tanks.
  • Fuel supply for turbines and large diesel engines.
  • Heavy equipment and fleet fuel.
  • Light fuel oils and other fuels, reviewed individually.

Fuel type, tank access, and site safety requirements are reviewed before service. Heavily degraded fuel or fuel with microbial growth may also need tank cleaning or other treatment.

Oil Sampling & Analysis

Understand your oil before deciding what comes next.

Oil sampling helps identify contamination and evaluate whether the oil is suitable for continued use, treatment, or replacement.

Depending on the application, assessment may include visual inspection, patch testing, and laboratory analysis.

Available laboratory testing can include

  • Particle count and ISO 4406 cleanliness reporting.
  • Water content by Karl Fischer.
  • Viscosity at 40°C.
  • Acid number (TAN).
  • FTIR analysis.
  • ICP elemental analysis for wear metals and selected contaminants.

The testing scope is selected for the oil, equipment, and maintenance objective. Visual appearance alone does not confirm oil condition.

Inside our systems

Industrial-grade equipment. Built to remove what damages your oil and fuel.

Every service runs on purpose-built equipment. Here is what each system does and why it matters for your operation.

01 · Vacuum dehydration

Removes water in every form.

Under vacuum, water evaporates at a much lower temperature, so it can be removed without overheating the oil. The process targets free, emulsified, and dissolved water, and it also pulls out entrained air and gases.

  • Helps the oil keep its viscosity and lubricating film.
  • Helps reduce rust, corrosion, and oxidation caused by water.
  • Suitable for turbine, hydraulic, compressor, gear, and other compatible oils.
  • Can run continuously while the equipment operates, where the setup allows.

02 · High-efficiency filtration

Captures particles you can’t see.

We use pleated microglass filter elements rated Beta(c) ≥ 1000 per ISO 16889, which means 99.9% capture at the rated micron size. Micron ratings range from 2.5 to 22, and bag pre-filters can be added for heavily contaminated fluid to protect the finer elements.

  • Progress is measured with ISO 4406 cleanliness codes.
  • Helps reduce wear on pumps, valves, bearings, and servo valves.
  • Element size is selected for each fluid and cleanliness target.

03 · High-flow flushing skids

Turbulent flow, adjusted to each circuit.

Our flushing skids pair high-flow pumps with variable frequency drives, so flow can be set for each circuit. Depending on the unit, options include an oil heater, inline particle monitoring with an ISO-format readout, and a reverse-flow manifold.

  • Flow set to create turbulent conditions in piping.
  • Extra particle removal for tanks, sumps, and reservoirs.
  • Particle protection for dehydrators and critical equipment.
  • Transfer pumping with filtration when oil is moved.

04 · Fuel filter & coalescer

Clean, dry fuel.

Fuel first passes through high-efficiency elements that capture particles. Coalescer elements then merge fine water droplets, separator elements keep them out of the clean fuel, and the water settles to the bottom of the vessel to be drained.

  • Helps protect fuel injectors on generators and diesel engines.
  • Helps prevent premature plugging of filters in the field.
  • Helps fuel meet the cleanliness levels engine and turbine makers specify.

Did you know?

New oil isn’t always clean oil.

New oil can arrive with more particles than your equipment manufacturer recommends. Filtering it as it goes into the system helps your equipment start clean.

FAQ

Questions plants ask us.

Can all used oil be restored?

No. Suitability depends on the oil's condition, contamination, additive health, and equipment requirements. Some oils require replacement.

Do we need to stop production?

It depends on the machine and service. Some filtration and dehydration projects can be performed while equipment operates. Other projects require a shutdown.

What is the difference between filtration and dehydration?

Filtration targets solid particles. Dehydration targets water contamination. The recommended treatment depends on the findings.

What is oil purification?

Oil purification removes contamination from oil that is still suitable for use. Our process combines particle filtration with vacuum dehydration to reduce solids, water, and entrained air and gases. A pre-treatment assessment confirms whether the oil is a candidate.

What is high-velocity oil flushing?

It is flushing at flow rates above normal operating flow to create turbulent flow, which helps carry particles out of piping and components into filtration. It is commonly used after new installations, component replacement, or contamination events.

Do you filter diesel fuel?

Yes. We filter diesel and other suitable fuels in storage tanks, day tanks, and supply systems, using high-efficiency filter elements plus coalescer and separator elements that remove free water. We review the fuel type, tank access, and site requirements first.

Should new oil be filtered before use?

Often, yes. New oil can contain more particles than the equipment manufacturer recommends. Filtering it as it is added to the system, or soon after, helps the equipment start with cleaner oil. A particle count can confirm the starting condition.

Is oil treatment the same as oil flushing?

No. Oil treatment focuses on the condition of the oil. Flushing focuses on removing contamination from the agreed system or circuit by circulating a compatible fluid through filtration equipment.

Can oil flushing be used on fuel or gas lines?

Our oil flushing offering is scoped for suitable oil circuits. Fuel, gas, and process piping require a separate engineering review to define an appropriate cleaning method and fluid.

Do you test the oil before treatment?

We require a pre-treatment oil assessment and define the sampling and testing scope for the application.

How often should oil be sampled or treated?

Frequency depends on operating conditions, equipment requirements, contamination exposure, and analysis trends.

Can you repair hydraulic leaks?

Yes. We offer inspection and repair subject to the component, access, parts availability, and agreed scope.

Do you supply the oil, filters, and replacement parts?

Responsibilities are defined in the proposal. Please share your fluid specifications, filter requirements, and repair needs when requesting service.

How is pricing determined?

Pricing depends on the service, oil volume and condition, equipment configuration, testing requirements, location, and project duration.
